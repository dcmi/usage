### DCTERMS Accessibility Properties

Previously proposed text for public comment was
- https://github.com/dcmi/dc-srap/issues/103#issuecomment-4878025815

ACTION 2026-10-13: SRAP WG to provide accessibility usage examples.

Tom: I still picture a property that takes a URI bc uncontrolled literals are counterproductive; had long call with Liddy Neville who was involved with accessibility since the 2000s who said that uncontrolled literals are not enough to select resources as needed to ensure accessibility.
Karen was disagreeing since this is not current practice (ie, we use rangeIncludes).

Tom: only vocabularies that are standard-like right now are schema.org vocabularies but they cover seven different accessibility properties; with literals there would no way for consumers to see the connection to controlled vocabularies

Karen: There were responses in https://github.com/dcmi/dc-srap/issues/133
In fact, we've got dozens of examples of people using their own term lists.
I don't know of anyone using URIs.

Tom: Karen, did you not suggest having a BIBO property for that?

Karen: It would be the only addition to DC terms that requires a URI, that would be pretty extreme.

Osma: I think we could use rangeIncludes here as well (lots of properties used to require URIs but were relaxed)

Karen: We hope that eventually there will be URIs but they are not here today and we can't just make them happen by defining that property.
On Jan 30 there is a comment that they would just move it to accessibilityNote; and someone from EPUB arguing for URIs but he is EPUB which is very different from EPUB community.
Another comment from Feb 4: preferable to allow literal values

Tom: but we went out with accessibilityNote only but in our latest draft we would have two properties, one to be used with URIs and one not.
Literal values from controlled vocabularies should be URIs to give the consumer the necessary context to work with them.

Karen: That's true; most repos are not really sharing data, they are for internal use, so nothing we do will change what people have available to them

Tom: National Library of Sweden uses URIs internally

Kalli: we talked about making that vocabulary more public, to be shared; there was an IFLA webinar last wekk, and every speaker was calling for more URIs, that what we have today is not working; if people are waiting for someone to do something about it, why can't it be us? why not change the paradigm, even is it will take a while?

Osma: if we publish a vocabulary we are responsible for maintaining it, that is a commitment

Tom: that is why Niklas is exploring it with his colleagues at KB, trying to clarify whether they feel comfortable with being exposed that way and taking it to the next stage

Karen: logically that should be an IFLA project

Osma: is it possible to declare the DC Terms accessibility property with a bit of hopium to take URIs, using rangeIncludes, and just see how it goes? how people will be using it?

Tom: and provide it with a bit of a warning that when used with literals, consumers will not have all the context; derefencerability

Osma: that could be mentioned in the usage note, reminds me of SKOS notation properties, are also particular to some encoding scheme, "use a data type that is specific to your scope"; describing this as a possible pattern if you do not use URIs

Tom: there was some idea for a way to make it easy to create a URI but it would be really hard to maintain, and legal issues when creating URIs about other people's work, so the idea was dropped
I am curious about the use of structured value, a node's neighbourhood is a structured value (specification from the late 1990s, Simon Cox et al.)

Karen: sounds like a blank node

Osma: but it's a string -- maybe the comparison is not the best

Karen: the dot notation in Dublin Core is also somewhat structured; people have been using structured values but we need to revise how to say that that is not the best way to do it.

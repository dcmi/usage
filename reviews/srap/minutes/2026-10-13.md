### DCTERMS Accessibility Properties

Previously proposed text for public comment was
- https://github.com/dcmi/dc-srap/issues/103#issuecomment-4878025815

Discussion if and how to move this forward

Tom: Osma argued that there are good reasons to postpone publishing SRAP because accessibility is a good example for design choices; looks to me that we could probably quickly approve the free-text property but that is not very satisfactory, we could do better; for the other one, there are no standard URI-based vocabularies after 20 years of accessibility work; National Libary of Sweden good example of in-house activities but can we point to that? can we encourage them and other institutions (Stanford, Finland) to come out with something (statement that they are at least experimenting) we can point to? it's a tall order, it means getting one or more institutions to take a stand and to commit but as long as this is not happening it will be problematic for us to go out on a limb

Niklas: from KB perspective we don't want to even indicate that we intend to do this alone, would have to be a collaboration to make sure the vocabulary reaches the right audience; there is a W3 group WCAG (?), maybe I will attend and take the temperature there; bringing a couple of institutions there could finally connect the dots betweens terms from schema.org with specific properties ("poor man's enumeration") and more concise structural and multilingual definitions, so that providers of documents can present them in a standardized way; we have an activity with another government agency in Sweden which is tasked with making sure that accessibility standards are implemented; we just took the term definitions from schema.org and asked if those are the ones that consumers need and could not answer that but now we are slowly connecting to representatives of the right communities; publishing something in a community group would probably be much quicker; how much should we say publicly about committing to schema.org?

Tom: declaring any dependency on schema.org in 2026 is problematic; schema.org is no longer a central part of their vision; Google has a bad track record with projects; why not take the same work and package it as something more general with URIs?

Karen: An accessibility property that works like 'subject' could be added to Dublin Core, then in BIBO we could have subproperties that work with text lists / URI lists. People that only use text, how do they do it? comma-separated?

Tom: Result from comment period is that free-text properties are useless/counter-productive; if you have a property which takes literals, that is a free-text property? except if we had encoding schemes which is not ideal either; answer has to be to use URIs; using properties that cater to different vocabularies is not sustainable

Karen: we will have to allow people to put in their own terms; people have chosen that and are doing it right now

Discussion: Do lists of terms equal free text?

Argie: would consider it free text if it possible to make a typo (which then hampers validation)

Niklas: at KB every pick list is URI-based

Karen: that is very unusual

Niklas: and they are all correlated with international standards, and we need to make that happen here as well, otherwise people will not start doing it

Tom: which is why -- see proposal above; W3C needs to give those items URIs; KB cannot maintain those

Karen: what do you think of my proposition wrt adding one property to DC Terms, and then a subproperty in BIBO that requires a URI? would satisfy both views

Tom: interesting but clashes a bit with the fact that it would essentially make it a free-text field which was criticized in public comments

Karen: need to check if that was intention of comments; and I am not saying we should have a different property for each pick list

Niklas: Gregorio Pellegrino discussed about this at length

Tom: we did not go out publicly with a URI-based property

Karen: then people probably did not include pick lists in what they meant by free text, they probably did not know that these lists even exist

Niklas: pick lists do not provide context, reference; and those pick lists are based on something

Karen: people use DC terms all the time without URIs! with dot etc.

Niklas: but those are all precursors to RDF; some of them are using namespace in XML

Tom: we would need another comment period to ask more explicitly about pick lists vs free text vs. URIs, and how people want to use them

Karen: I think that accessibility can been part of DC terms, somewhat useless like "subject", and for more specific things people use BIBO

Niklas: we should discuss with the larger group, and also find examples from different repositories

ACTION: add that to the agenda
